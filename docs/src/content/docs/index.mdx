## Where jobs live

A worker runs its jobs the same way wherever the queue keeps them. Memory is built in. The redisq and sqliteq adapters keep jobs in Redis or SQLite, so they survive a restart, and Redis lets several programs share one queue.

</div>

## Performance

Measured on an i7-13700 (24 threads) on 2026-10-25. Charts, raw results and the harness are at [benchmarks.goptics.org](https://benchmarks.goptics.org).

The [comparison](/reference/comparison/) lists features side by side with other Go worker pools.

</div>

<CardGrid>
	<Card title="4.89M tasks/s">
		Mean of 15 runs of 1M tasks (3 task types by 5 producer shapes) at GOMAXPROCS 24, 8 and 4, with
		50K workers. The next fastest pool, a hand-rolled buffered channel pool, does 2.73M, and ants 2.22M. It uses more memory: 115 MB per 1M
		tasks against 15 MB for ants, because `Add` never blocks.
	</Card>
	<Card title="59,898 jobs/s on Redis">
		With redisq, one instance with 100 workers, moving 100K no-op jobs from 100 producers. With 10
		workers taking one job at a time, the median time from enqueue to handler is 0.058 ms.
	</Card>
	<Card title="14,725 jobs/s on SQLite">
		With sqliteq, draining 20K jobs on WAL. goqite does 2,933 and River 3,230 on the same run.
	</Card>
</CardGrid>
