An in-memory queue is gone when the process exits. A persistent or distributed queue keeps its jobs in storage outside the process, behind a storage adapter. varmq has no adapter of its own. The two that exist are separate modules: [redisq](https://github.com/goptics/redisq) for Redis and [sqliteq](https://github.com/goptics/sqliteq) for SQLite.

This page covers what the two kinds of queue do in varmq. [Redis](/persistence/redis/) and [SQLite](/persistence/sqlite/) show the setup and what each adapter does with a job that was not finished. [Adapters](/persistence/adapters/) lists the interfaces, for reading an adapter or writing one.

## Persistent or distributed

You bind both kinds to a worker the way you bind an in-memory queue. Only a plain worker from [`NewWorker`](https://pkg.go.dev/github.com/goptics/varmq#NewWorker) can bind them. An error worker or a result worker binds in-memory queues only.

| | Persistent | Distributed |
|---|---|---|
| Bind with | [`WithPersistentQueue`](https://pkg.go.dev/github.com/goptics/varmq#IWorkerBinder.WithPersistentQueue), [`WithPersistentPriorityQueue`](https://pkg.go.dev/github.com/goptics/varmq#IWorkerBinder.WithPersistentPriorityQueue) | [`WithDistributedQueue`](https://pkg.go.dev/github.com/goptics/varmq#IWorkerBinder.WithDistributedQueue), [`WithDistributedPriorityQueue`](https://pkg.go.dev/github.com/goptics/varmq#IWorkerBinder.WithDistributedPriorityQueue) |
| The storage must implement | [`IPersistentQueue`](https://pkg.go.dev/github.com/goptics/varmq#IPersistentQueue), [`IPersistentPriorityQueue`](https://pkg.go.dev/github.com/goptics/varmq#IPersistentPriorityQueue) | [`IDistributedQueue`](https://pkg.go.dev/github.com/goptics/varmq#IDistributedQueue), [`IDistributedPriorityQueue`](https://pkg.go.dev/github.com/goptics/varmq#IDistributedPriorityQueue): the persistent ones plus [`ISubscribable`](https://pkg.go.dev/github.com/goptics/varmq#ISubscribable) |
| You get back | [`PersistentQueue[T]`](https://pkg.go.dev/github.com/goptics/varmq#PersistentQueue) or [`PersistentPriorityQueue[T]`](https://pkg.go.dev/github.com/goptics/varmq#PersistentPriorityQueue), which has [`Worker()`](https://pkg.go.dev/github.com/goptics/varmq#PersistentQueue.Worker) | [`DistributedQueue[T]`](https://pkg.go.dev/github.com/goptics/varmq#DistributedQueue) or [`DistributedPriorityQueue[T]`](https://pkg.go.dev/github.com/goptics/varmq#DistributedPriorityQueue), which has no `Worker()` |
| Jobs survive a restart | as long as the storage keeps them | as long as the storage keeps them |
| The worker learns of new jobs | when something in its own process signals it: an [`Add`](https://pkg.go.dev/github.com/goptics/varmq#PersistentQueue.Add), a start or resume, or one of its jobs finishing | the same, and when the storage notifies it |
| A process that only adds jobs | not provided | [`NewDistributedQueue`](https://pkg.go.dev/github.com/goptics/varmq#NewDistributedQueue) and [`NewDistributedPriorityQueue`](https://pkg.go.dev/github.com/goptics/varmq#NewDistributedPriorityQueue) wrap the storage with no worker |

The row about learning of new jobs is the one that decides which to use. A persistent worker pulls when something in its own process signals it: an `Add`, a start or resume, or one of its own jobs finishing. With redisq and sqliteq, nothing signals it when another process adds a job to the same storage, so it may not look until one of those happens. Use a persistent queue when one process wants its jobs to survive a restart.

A distributed queue adds [`Subscribe`](https://pkg.go.dev/github.com/goptics/varmq#ISubscribable.Subscribe). The worker subscribes when you bind the queue, and the adapter calls the worker back with the action `"enqueued"` when a job is added. That wakes the worker to pull. It is how several processes can consume one queue, with each job going to one of them. [Adapters](/persistence/adapters/#isubscribable) has the rest of the contract.

## What is stored

varmq stores each job as JSON, in the shape `{"id":"","status":"Created","data":"job-1"}`. `data` is what you passed to `Add`. That has two consequences:

- **The payload type has to survive `encoding/json`.** `Add` returns `false` when JSON encoding fails, for example for a function, so [function helpers](/guides/function-helpers/) cannot be used here.
- **Producers and consumers have to agree on the type.** A consumer decodes the stored JSON into its own `T`. Bytes that do not decode are reported as [`ErrParseJob`](https://pkg.go.dev/github.com/goptics/varmq#ErrParseJob) on [`Errs()`](https://pkg.go.dev/github.com/goptics/varmq#Worker.Errs) and to the [error handler](/guides/errors/#the-error-handler). The worker does not acknowledge such a job, so what happens to it next is up to the adapter.

`Add` on these queues returns only a `bool`. It is `false` when the encoding fails or when the storage refuses the job, for example because the queue was closed. There is no job handle, so there is nothing to [`Wait`](https://pkg.go.dev/github.com/goptics/varmq#Awaitable.Wait) on and no [`Err`](https://pkg.go.dev/github.com/goptics/varmq#EnqueuedErrJob.Err) or [`Result`](https://pkg.go.dev/github.com/goptics/varmq#EnqueuedResultJob.Result) to read, and there is no [`AddAll`](https://pkg.go.dev/github.com/goptics/varmq#Queue.AddAll). [Worker types](/guides/worker-types/#which-queues-each-worker-can-bind) has the table.

The handle's [`Len`](https://pkg.go.dev/github.com/goptics/varmq#IExternalBaseQueue.Len) asks the storage, so it counts the jobs waiting there, including jobs another process added. [`WithQueueCapacity`](https://pkg.go.dev/github.com/goptics/varmq#WithQueueCapacity) reaches the storage only if the adapter implements [`CapacitySetter`](https://pkg.go.dev/github.com/goptics/varmq#CapacitySetter). Neither redisq nor sqliteq does. With them `Add` is not refused, and [`IsFull`](https://pkg.go.dev/github.com/goptics/varmq#IExternalBaseQueue.IsFull) only compares `Len` with the number you passed.

## Acknowledgement

An in-memory job is done when its function returns. A job in storage needs one more step, so that storage knows the job was not lost on the way.

1. When the worker has a free slot, it takes a job with [`DequeueWithAckId`](https://pkg.go.dev/github.com/goptics/varmq#IAcknowledgeable.DequeueWithAckId). The storage hands the job over together with an acknowledgement ID, and keeps its own record that the job is out.
2. The job's function runs.
3. When the function returns, the worker closes the job, and closing it calls [`Acknowledge`](https://pkg.go.dev/github.com/goptics/varmq#IAcknowledgeable.Acknowledge) with that ID. The storage can now forget the job.

Three details about step 3:

- **A failed or panicking job is acknowledged too.** varmq recovers a panic, counts the job as failed and reports the panic. Then it acknowledges the job like any other. Nothing in varmq retries a job.
- **A job whose acknowledgement fails is reported.** If `Acknowledge` returns `false`, the worker sends [`ErrAcknowledgeJob`](https://pkg.go.dev/github.com/goptics/varmq#ErrAcknowledgeJob) to `Errs()` and the handler. The job's function has already run.
- **Taken jobs run.** A worker with these queues takes one job per free slot and starts it. A [`Pause`](https://pkg.go.dev/github.com/goptics/varmq#Worker.Pause) or [`Stop`](https://pkg.go.dev/github.com/goptics/varmq#Worker.Stop) does not park a job the worker already took, because it is unacknowledged and a long wait could let a visibility timeout hand it to another consumer. Call [`StopAndWait`](https://pkg.go.dev/github.com/goptics/varmq#Worker.StopAndWait) before you close the adapter's queue, so that running jobs can acknowledge.

## What this guarantees

varmq acknowledges a job it took from storage after the job's function has returned, and not before. So if the process dies while a job runs, the job was not acknowledged.

What the storage then does with it is the adapter's decision. varmq cannot make it come back. [Redis](/persistence/redis/#jobs-that-were-not-acknowledged) and [SQLite](/persistence/sqlite/#jobs-that-were-not-acknowledged) say what redisq and sqliteq do. With either adapter the best case is at-least-once delivery:

- **A job can run twice.** With these adapters, a job whose process died mid-run is handed out again, and so is a job whose acknowledgement failed after it ran. Write the job function so that running it twice is safe.
- **A job can be lost with some adapters.** An adapter that takes a job and records it as in flight in two separate steps can lose it if the process dies between them. redisq and sqliteq do both in one step.
- **varmq does not deduplicate for you.** [`WithJobId`](https://pkg.go.dev/github.com/goptics/varmq#WithJobId) puts an ID in the stored JSON. Storage that implements [`IDedupQueue`](https://pkg.go.dev/github.com/goptics/varmq#IDedupQueue) can use it to store a retried `Add` once, which stops a double add and says nothing about a double run. redisq and sqliteq both implement it.

## Starting a worker on a non-empty queue

After a restart, jobs from the earlier run are still in storage. Bind the queue first and start the worker afterwards:

```go
worker := varmq.NewWorker(process, varmq.WithAutoRun(false))
queue := worker.WithPersistentQueue(pq)
worker.Start()
```

A worker that starts on creation, which is the default, signals its event loop to look for work before you have bound the queue. If the loop reads that signal first, it finds no queue. Unless the adapter signals on bind, as below, nothing signals it again until something in this process does, as listed above, so the old jobs can sit in storage until then. With [`WithAutoRun(false)`](https://pkg.go.dev/github.com/goptics/varmq#WithAutoRun), [`Start`](https://pkg.go.dev/github.com/goptics/varmq#Worker.Start) runs after the bind and signals the loop itself. [Lifecycle](/concepts/lifecycle/#withautorun) covers the option.

Adapters that implement [`IBatchAcknowledgeable`](https://pkg.go.dev/github.com/goptics/varmq#IBatchAcknowledgeable) signal the worker when you bind them. redisq and sqliteq both do, so with them the order does not matter.

## Metrics

[Metrics](/guides/metrics/) describes the four counters for in-memory queues. For these queues, keep three things in mind.

- **[`Submitted`](https://pkg.go.dev/github.com/goptics/varmq#Metrics.Submitted) on a persistent queue counts this process's accepted `Add` calls.** A job that was already in storage, or that another process added, is not counted there. It still counts in [`Completed`](https://pkg.go.dev/github.com/goptics/varmq#Metrics.Completed) when this worker runs it, so `Completed` can be higher than `Submitted`.
- **The counters of a worker on a distributed queue may differ from what you expect.** `Submitted` in particular. These docs do not cover how a distributed queue's counters count yet.
- **[`NumPending`](https://pkg.go.dev/github.com/goptics/varmq#Worker.NumPending) asks the storage.** It is the number of jobs waiting there, plus jobs the worker has taken and not yet started. The queue handle's `Len` leaves out the second kind. Each call is a call to the storage.

## Persistent topics are not released

[Topics](/guides/topics/) are in-memory only. A persistent topic was built and then moved out of the main branch before release, in [#110](https://github.com/goptics/varmq/pull/110). It lives on the [`exp/persistent-topic`](https://github.com/goptics/varmq/tree/exp/persistent-topic) branch, it is not part of v1.10.0, and these pages do not describe it. To share jobs between processes, use a distributed queue.
