Every worker keeps three counters over its lifetime. [`Metrics()`](https://pkg.go.dev/github.com/goptics/varmq#Worker.Metrics) returns them. They tell you how many jobs the worker ran and how they ended, without tracking jobs yourself.

## The three counters

[`Metrics`](https://pkg.go.dev/github.com/goptics/varmq#Metrics) has three read methods, each returning a `uint64`:

| Method | Counts |
|---|---|
| [`Completed()`](https://pkg.go.dev/github.com/goptics/varmq#Metrics.Completed) | jobs whose function has returned, whether it succeeded or failed. |
| [`Successful()`](https://pkg.go.dev/github.com/goptics/varmq#Metrics.Successful) | jobs whose function returned without an error and without a panic. A plain worker's function has no error to return, so for it this means it returned normally. |
| [`Failed()`](https://pkg.go.dev/github.com/goptics/varmq#Metrics.Failed) | jobs whose function returned an error, or panicked. |

A job that runs counts once in `Completed` and once in `Successful` or `Failed`. After a worker has gone idle, `Completed` equals `Successful` plus `Failed`, unless you called [`Reset`](https://pkg.go.dev/github.com/goptics/varmq#Metrics.Reset) partway.

Only a job that ran counts. A job closed or [cancelled](/concepts/jobs/#cancelling-a-job) before it starts counts in none of them. A job removed by [`Purge`](https://pkg.go.dev/github.com/goptics/varmq#IExternalBaseQueue.Purge), or a topic payload that is dropped unread, does not count either.

## Jobs a queue received

A worker does not count the jobs its queues receive. Each queue counts them itself, because several workers or processes can share one queue. Read the count as `totals.submitted` from [`GET /queues`](/operations/rest-api/#queues-and-jobs) in the REST API. [Lifecycle](/concepts/lifecycle/#what-happens-to-jobs) explains how the jobs that wait relate to `NumPending` and `Len`.

- **The counters are cumulative.** They start at zero when the worker is created, and nothing but `Reset` clears them. Pausing, stopping, restarting and purging do not change them.
- **They are safe to read from any goroutine.** Each method is one atomic read.
- **Three reads are not one snapshot.** A job can finish between two of them. So three values read while jobs run can be off by as many jobs as finish between the reads.
- **A job's own [`Wait`](https://pkg.go.dev/github.com/goptics/varmq#Awaitable.Wait) can return a moment before `Completed` counts it.** The worker closes a finished job, which releases `Wait`. Just after that, the worker counts the job as completed. `Successful` and `Failed` are already counted by then. To read settled values, wait for the worker with [`WaitUntilIdle`](https://pkg.go.dev/github.com/goptics/varmq#Worker.WaitUntilIdle) or [`Wait`](https://pkg.go.dev/github.com/goptics/varmq#Worker.Wait) first. [Waiting](/guides/waiting/) compares them.

## Reset

[`Reset`](https://pkg.go.dev/github.com/goptics/varmq#Metrics.Reset) sets all three counters to zero. It does not touch the queue or the jobs that are running.

- The three counters are cleared one after another, not in one step.
- A job that was waiting or running when you call it is counted when it finishes. So after a `Reset` in the middle of a run, the counters do not stay at zero.
