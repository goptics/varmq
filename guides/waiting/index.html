Waiting

Add returns at once, and the worker runs the job later. A set of calls on the worker block until the worker as a whole reaches a point: drained, paused or stopped. They do not wait for one job. For that, use the job’s own handle (waiting on a job).

Wait and WaitUntilIdle

Section titled “Wait and WaitUntilIdle”

Wait and WaitUntilIdle both wait for the worker to finish its work. They differ in what they ask of the worker’s status:

Worker’s status Wait returns when WaitUntilIdle returns when
Running or Idle no job is pending and none is running the same, and the status is Idle
Pausing, Paused, Stopping or Stopped no job is running, even if jobs are still pending the worker is Idle again, with nothing pending
Initiated at once, even if jobs are queued the worker has been started and drained

The godoc puts the difference this way: Wait “does NOT require the status to be Idle”. It answers “is the work done or halted?”, and WaitUntilIdle answers “is the worker idle with nothing left?”.

In practice:

  • On a worker that keeps running, both return when it has drained.
  • If something may pause or stop the worker, Wait returns when the running jobs have finished, and jobs that have not started stay queued. WaitUntilIdle keeps waiting through a pause or a stop, until a later Resume, Restart or Start brings the worker back to Idle and it drains.
  • A worker created with WithAutoRun(false) is Initiated until you call Start. Wait on it returns at once, with every job still queued. Start the worker first.

Both cover everything bound to the worker: each queue and each topic subscription. They return at a moment when the worker is empty, so a job added afterwards is not waited for.


Caution
Do not call Wait, WaitUntilIdle or the combined calls below on a worker from inside that worker’s own function. The job that is calling counts as running, so the call waits for the job it is in.


Waiting for a state
Section titled “Waiting for a state”


WaitUntilPaused and WaitUntilStopped wait for one status and look at nothing else. They return at once if the worker is already there. Otherwise they keep waiting until something puts it there, so a worker that you stop while you wait for Paused leaves the call waiting.


Pause and Stop return as soon as the change has started. While a job is running, the worker is Pausing or Stopping. Call WaitUntilPaused or WaitUntilStopped after them to wait until the change is over. The lifecycle page lists the states. Its first program shows a worker go from Pausing to Paused.


Combined calls
Section titled “Combined calls”


Four calls join a wait and a pause or a stop. The verb that comes first happens first.






Call
Does
Returns when






WaitAndPause
Wait, then Pause
Pause has been called. The worker can still be Pausing.




PauseAndWait
Pause, then WaitUntilPaused
the worker is Paused




WaitAndStop
Wait, then Stop
Stop has been called. The worker can still be Stopping.




StopAndWait
Stop, then WaitUntilStopped
the worker is Stopped






Each returns an error: the one Pause or Stop returns. PauseAndWait and StopAndWait return it without waiting when the pause or stop failed. The lifecycle page lists the errors.


WaitAndStop drains, StopAndWait does not. On a worker that is running, WaitAndStop lets every pending job run, then starts the stop. A job added after Wait returns can still be left queued, so stop adding jobs before you call it. (On a paused worker, Wait returns with jobs still pending, and they stay queued.) It returns before the worker has reached Stopped, so call WaitUntilStopped after it if you need that. StopAndWait starts the stop at once and blocks until the worker is Stopped. Running jobs finish, and jobs that had not started stay queued: NumPending counts them, and they run after a Restart. See what happens to jobs.

NumPending and NumProcessing
Section titled “NumPending and NumProcessing”


Two methods on Worker count the work in flight:


    

  • NumPending is the number of jobs that have not started: the jobs waiting in the bound queues, the jobs a goroutine has taken into a batch but not started, and the jobs parked by a pause or stop. A queue’s own Len() counts only the first group. A job you closed while it waited still counts until the worker reaches it. While a goroutine works through a batch, the batch’s finished jobs still count until the whole batch ends, so mid-run it can read high. It is exact once the worker is idle, paused or stopped.
    • 

  • NumProcessing is the number of jobs running right now. It is at most the concurrency limit, except for a while after TunePool lowers it.
    • 



Each is read on its own, so the two together are not a snapshot of one moment. They suit a progress display or a backpressure check. To know that the work is finished, use Wait. For counters over the worker’s lifetime, see metrics.
